Mixed ages mean every house gets its own assessment

A club that opened in 1968 and subdivisions built in later decades sit under one local name. That means homes in the Buckhorn area can differ in age, roof type and floor plan. Age alone does not tell us how much water a home has taken in, so we measure before we decide.

A technician checks the wet area with a moisture meter, then checks the walls, ceiling and floor around it. Water often travels along framing and pipes, so a stain in one room can come from a leak somewhere else.

First steps while you wait for help

Stop the water if it is safe

Turn off the supply to the leaking fixture. If water comes from the roof, place buckets and move furniture away from drips.

Keep people and pets away from hazards

Stay out of rooms with standing water near outlets. Do not use electrical devices while standing in water.

Document the damage

Take photos of the source, the wet areas and damaged items before you move them.

Call with the details

Tell us the address, the source if you know it and how long water has been present.

Heavy rain and the wet season

The National Weather Service in Tampa Bay defines the local wet season as running from late May to early October. During those months, storms can bring roof leaks and water at doors. County and regional weather information does not describe damage in any single subdivision, so we do not assume it.

If a tree limb or storm opens the roof, a roofer can tarp it while we handle the interior. Keep a few photos of the opening, the debris and the wet rooms for your records.

Drying and records

After water is removed, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure. We take readings at the start and at each visit, so you and your insurer can see how the drying progressed. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water damage restoration, and we use it as a guide, not as a certification claim. Your insurer decides coverage under your policy, and we do not promise any claim outcome.
