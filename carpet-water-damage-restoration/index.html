Carpet and padding soak up water like a sponge. Pull back furniture, blot standing water and call our 24/7 line. We extract the water, check the padding and subfloor and decide whether the carpet can be dried in place, lifted and reset, or replaced.

What decides whether carpet can be saved

The source of the water matters most. Clean water from a supply line leaves carpet salvageable if it is dried promptly. Water from a sewage backup or flood is contaminated and carpet and padding are usually removed.

Water sourceTypical result
Clean supply line or applianceDry in place if caught early.
Rain through the roofOften dried in place, with padding checked.
Flood or surface waterCarpet and padding usually removed.
Sewage backupCarpet and padding removed.

How long the carpet stayed wet also changes the result.

Padding is the problem layer

Foam padding holds water and dries slowly under the carpet. Many jobs involve replacing the padding while the carpet is lifted, dried and reinstalled. A new pad often costs less than redoing the whole job later.

Wet padding also holds odors and can grow mold. If it stays wet for days, replacement is the safer choice.

Extract

Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull water out of the carpet and padding.

Lift and inspect

We peel back the carpet from tack strips and check the pad and subfloor.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run, with meters checking the subfloor.

Reinstall or replace

Carpet is stretched and reset, or replaced if it cannot recover.

Signs the carpet needs attention

  • A musty smell that gets stronger when it is humid.
  • Rippling or loose areas near the wall.
  • Stains or discoloration spreading from the edges.
  • Baseboards that stain or lift.
  • A squishy feeling underfoot after the surface looks dry.

If a flood or sewage backup caused the damage, see flood damage restoration or sewage backup cleanup. For slow drying beneath carpet, structural drying explains how moisture is tracked.

What affects repair scope and cost

  • The size of the area and how many rooms are wet.
  • The type of carpet and padding.
  • The source of the water and whether it was contaminated.
  • How long it stayed wet before extraction.
  • Whether the subfloor or tack strips need work.
  • Replacement and installation if carpet cannot be saved.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and you decide before we begin. Photograph wet areas and keep samples of any damaged carpet for your insurer. The Florida Department of Financial Services advises reporting a claim promptly and contacting your insurer before signing a repair contract.

Reinstalling carpet after drying

When carpet can be saved, it is lifted at the walls, the pad is replaced and the carpet is stretched back onto the tack strips. A power stretcher takes out ripples so the carpet lies flat again. Seams may need to be re-glued or re-sewn if they pulled apart while wet.

Ask for the old padding to be discarded and replaced with a new pad rather than being reused, because wet padding rarely recovers its cushioning.

Carpet water damage questions from Valrico homeowners

How quickly must carpet be dried?

Within a day or two is best. After that, odors, staining and mold risk grow and replacement becomes more likely.

Can wet carpet be dried without lifting it?

Sometimes. Surface extraction and drying under the carpet can work for lighter damage, but heavier water usually means lifting the carpet to remove the padding.

Will the smell go away?

If the carpet and padding are dried properly and any contaminated material is removed, odors usually go. Persistent smells mean moisture or contamination remains.
