Questions to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company

When water is on the floor, you have little time to compare. These questions sort the careful from the careless.

By the Valrico Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 12, 2026.

24/7 dispatch
(813) 738-0233 Call Now

Choosing a restoration company in the middle of a water emergency is stressful. Water is spreading, your insurer is waiting for a call and you have no time to research. A short list of questions can tell you a lot in a five minute phone call.

These questions apply to any company, including us. We think you should hear clear answers from everyone you consider.

Questions about the work

  1. What will you do first, and what will happen next?
  2. How will you decide what is wet and what is dry?
  3. Will you use moisture meters and keep daily readings?
  4. What equipment will you place, and how long do you expect it to run?
  5. What will you remove, and what can be saved?
  6. How will you handle contaminated water or sewage?

A good answer is specific. You should hear about meters, readings and a drying goal, not just that fans will run for a few days. See structural drying for what a measured plan looks like.

Questions about price

  • How is the price set, and what does it include?
  • Will I know the scope and the price before work starts?
  • What could change the scope, and how will you tell me?
  • Is the equipment priced per day, per job or per room?
  • What is not included, such as repairs or replacement?
  • Will you provide a written scope?

Be cautious of anyone who says the price cannot be explained until the work is finished. We explain the scope and how the price is set before work starts, and you decide before any equipment goes down.

Questions about documentation and insurance

Photos and logs

Ask what records you will receive, such as photos, readings and daily logs.

Claim help

Ask what your insurer will need and how the company supports the claim.

Billing

Ask whether you pay directly or the company bills your insurer.

Authorization

Ask what you are signing before you sign, and keep a copy.

The Florida Department of Financial Services recommends contacting your insurer before signing a repair contract, so call them first if you can.

Questions about licenses and insurance

Ask whether the company is licensed and insured, and ask for the details that apply to the work. For mold work, Florida licenses assessors and remediators as individuals, so ask for the license of the person who will do the work.

Water mitigation, mold remediation and rebuilding can involve different licenses and different people. Ask who will perform each part.

Questions about response and availability

  • Is the line answered at night and on weekends?
  • Who will arrive, and how will they identify themselves?
  • Do you give estimated arrival times, and what do they depend on?
  • What happens if the job is larger than expected?
  • Who is my contact during the job?

Be wary of firm arrival promises during a storm or a regional event. Honest companies tell you what they can do and update you as conditions change.

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign immediately without explaining the scope.
  • No written scope or pricing explanation.
  • No mention of moisture readings.
  • Claims to do work that requires licenses they cannot show.
  • Asking for a large payment up front with no details.
  • Refusing to explain what will be removed.

If you are weighing a restoration company against a handyman, the difference is mostly measurement and documentation. See water extraction and burst pipe water damage for examples of what a full response includes.

What a good answer sounds like

You will know a company is serious when answers are specific and honest. They describe how they will find wet areas, what equipment they will use and what readings they will share. They explain what they do not know yet and how the scope could change.

Vague answers, pressure and promises about exact timelines deserve a second look. Take a few minutes to compare, even during an emergency.

According to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, the speed and method of drying decide how much of a structure can be saved, which is why this guide stresses acting early.

Local note on permits and records

Hillsborough County Development Services says cosmetic repairs such as drywall removal or replacement do not need a permit in the unincorporated county, while structural repairs inside a Special Flood Hazard Area do. Ask any company how permits and the county Substantial Damage Packet apply to your address, whether you live in Valrico, Dover or Tampa.

Hiring questions Valrico homeowners ask

How do I compare quotes from two companies?

Compare the scope first. One company may plan more drying days or more removal than another. A written scope makes the price meaningful.

Should I sign right away if the crew is on site?

Read what you are signing. Ask for a copy and for an explanation of anything unclear. Contact your insurer when you can.

Do I need to be home during the work?

Not always, but you should be reachable. Ask who will be on site and how you will be updated.

How do I check that drying is complete?

Ask for moisture readings on the affected materials and on unaffected materials in the same house. Drying is complete when they match.

What if the company finds more damage than expected?

A good company stops, explains what changed and gets your approval before expanding the scope.
