What to Keep or Toss After Water Damage: A Practical Guide

The right call depends on the water source and the material. Document first, then decide.

By the Valrico Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 12, 2026.

24/7 dispatch
(813) 738-0233 Call Now

After water damage, you face a pile of wet belongings and a hard question. Is this worth saving? Throw away too much and you lose things you could have cleaned. Keep too much and you bring contamination or mold back into the house.

The answer depends on two things, what the water was and what the item is made of. Clean supply water is more forgiving than flood or sewage water, and hard materials are more forgiving than porous ones.

Start with the water category

Water sourceWhat it means for belongings
Clean water from a supply line or applianceMany items can be dried and saved if acted on quickly.
Rain that came through the roofOften treated like clean water but check for contamination.
Flood or surface waterPorous items are usually discarded.
Sewage backupPorous items are discarded and hard items are cleaned and sanitized.

When in doubt, treat the water as contaminated.

Document before you discard

The Florida Department of Financial Services advises policyholders to photograph or video damaged items and to dispose of them only if they present a health risk. Make a list as you go, including a description, an approximate age and an approximate value.

  1. Photograph each item with the damage visible.
  2. Write a short description and estimated value.
  3. Keep samples of damaged flooring or fabric when asked by your insurer.
  4. Bag discarded items and record what went in each bag.
  5. Ask your adjuster before you throw away larger pieces.

Items that can often be saved

Hard furniture

Solid wood and metal pieces can often be cleaned and dried, though finishes may need work.

Glass, ceramics and metal

Wash and sanitize them. They are non-porous.

Some clothing and linens

Clean-water laundry can often be washed. Contaminated fabrics are usually discarded.

Photos and papers

Frozen or air-dried documents can sometimes be saved. See the separate guide for details.

Items that are usually discarded

  • Carpet and padding soaked by flood or sewage water.
  • Mattresses and upholstered furniture soaked by dirty water.
  • Particleboard furniture that has swollen.
  • Insulation and drywall that stayed wet.
  • Food and medicines exposed to floodwater.
  • Stuffed toys and pillows soaked by contaminated water.

When water has touched food or medicine, the safest approach is to discard them. We are not making medical claims, so ask a doctor or pharmacist about medications if you are unsure.

The building itself

Belongings are one part of the job. The structure needs drying too, and drying decisions come first because wet walls and floors damage contents again. See structural drying for how we verify a house is dry.

Wet carpet and padding often come out. Carpet water damage restoration explains how those decisions are made, and sewage backup cleanup covers contaminated cases.

When to ask a professional

  • The item is valuable or sentimental.
  • The water was contaminated and you are unsure.
  • The item is large, heavy or electrical.
  • You need documentation for an insurer.
  • You see mold or smell odors.

A simple labeling system for sorting

Set up three zones. Use one for items to keep, one for items to clean or dry and one for items to discard. Label bags and boxes with the room and a number, and keep a running list on paper or in a note on your phone.

This helps your insurer see what was lost, and it helps you remember what was in a room six weeks later.

Take breaks. Sorting through damaged belongings is tiring and emotional, and rushed decisions often lead to tossing something that mattered. If an item is sentimental and you are unsure, set it aside and ask a conservator or specialist later.

Ask your insurer which items need to be kept for inspection before you throw them out, and photograph everything before disposal. Keep a copy of the list somewhere dry, such as your phone or a cloud folder.

According to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, the speed and method of drying decide how much of a structure can be saved, which is why this guide stresses acting early.

Local note on claims

The Florida Department of Financial Services guide says to make emergency repairs and keep receipts, and to contact your insurer before signing a repair contract. It also says disposal should be limited to items that present a health risk, so photograph first. These steps apply to homeowners in Valrico, Lithia and Tampa alike.

Belongings questions Valrico homeowners ask

How long can wet items sit before they are ruined?

Within a day or two, mold and odor start to set in on porous materials. Move quickly to dry what you plan to keep.

Can I save a mattress that got wet?

Mattresses are porous and hard to clean. Clean-water cases sometimes dry, but dirty water usually means disposal. Ask your insurer.

Should I wash everything that got wet?

Wash clean-water laundry promptly. Items exposed to sewage or flood water should be discarded or professionally cleaned.

Do I need to keep damaged items for the adjuster?

Ask your adjuster. Photos and a list are usually enough, but some items may be requested for inspection.

Can you clean contents?

We focus on drying and restoring the structure. For belongings, ask your insurer about contents cleaning services, and ask us anything about the structure when you call.
