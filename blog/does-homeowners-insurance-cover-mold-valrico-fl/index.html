Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold in Florida?

Mold coverage is often limited, and it usually depends on what caused the moisture.

By the Valrico Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 12, 2026.

24/7 dispatch
(813) 738-0233 Call Now

Mold coverage is one of the most confusing parts of a homeowners policy. Some policies cover mold that results from a covered water loss, some cap the payout and some exclude mold entirely unless you buy an endorsement. The answer lives in your policy, and the answer from your insurer matters more than any general guide.

This article explains how the pieces usually fit together. We are not an insurer or an attorney and cannot say what your policy covers.

The cause of the moisture comes first

Insurers usually look at why the mold grew. If a covered event such as a sudden pipe burst caused the water, mold that followed may be treated as part of that loss. If mold grew from long-term seepage, humidity or poor maintenance, it is often excluded.

ScenarioHow it is often handled
Sudden pipe burst followed by moldMay be covered under the water loss, subject to limits.
Roof damage from a covered storm followed by moldMay be covered if a covered peril caused the water.
Long-term leak under a sinkOften excluded as gradual damage.
Mold from high humidity and no leakUsually not covered.
Mold after floodFlood is excluded from standard policies.

Read your policy for specific language and ask your insurer to confirm in writing.

Limits, sublimits and exclusions

Many policies include a limit on mold coverage that is much lower than the full policy limit. Some separate the testing, the removal and the repairs. Others exclude mold except where it results from a listed cause.

  • Ask whether mold has a separate limit.
  • Ask whether testing and removal are included.
  • Ask whether the limit is per claim or per policy year.
  • Ask whether additional coverage can be added.
  • Ask what documentation is required.
  • Ask how the insurer defines gradual damage.

How Florida claim timelines apply

The Florida Department of Financial Services guide to homeowners claims advises reporting the claim immediately and making and documenting emergency repairs. It also says to keep receipts and to contact the insurer before signing a repair contract.

Those rules matter for mold. Waiting to dry a wet house can create the mold that gets excluded, so quick action protects both the property and the claim. See structural drying for how moisture is tracked.

Documenting a mold claim

Photograph the area

Wide and close shots from several angles, with something for scale.

Record moisture readings

Meter readings on affected and unaffected materials show the water event.

Keep records of the water event

Dates, plumber invoices and repair receipts connect the mold to its cause.

Hold off on disposal

Photograph items before discarding unless they present a health risk.

We take moisture readings, photos and drying logs during water jobs. For mold that appears after a loss, mold remediation explains the scope.

Remediation and assessor rules

Florida licenses mold assessors and remediators as individuals, and a company that does both needs a separate license for each. An assessor may not remediate a structure that the same assessor or company assessed in the previous 12 months, and the reverse applies. Ask your insurer which documents it needs from each.

When coverage is denied or limited

  1. Ask for the decision and its reasons in writing.
  2. Compare the language to your policy.
  3. Ask your insurer to explain the limit or exclusion.
  4. Contact the Florida Department of Financial Services consumer help line if you need guidance.
  5. Keep dated copies of everything you submit.

A note on honest expectations

Mold claims often take longer than water claims because the insurer wants to understand the cause, the timeline and the scope. Keep your own notes about when you first noticed a smell or a stain, because those dates matter. Honest records help whether the claim is approved, limited or denied.

If you plan to sell, remember that buyers and lenders can ask about past mold even when insurance paid nothing. Keep any inspection reports and repair receipts with your home records.

According to the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation, controlling moisture comes first, and that guidance is why this guide keeps returning to drying and humidity.

Local note on timing

The Florida Department of Financial Services guide says the insurer should acknowledge a claim within 7 days and issue a written denial or payment of the undisputed portion within 60 days of the report. That timeline matters in Valrico, where humidity can turn a wet wall into a mold problem within days. If you live in Dover or Tampa, the same state timeline applies.

Mold insurance questions Valrico homeowners ask

Is mold covered if it came from a burst pipe?

Often yes, up to a limit, when the burst was sudden and covered. Read your policy and ask your insurer to confirm.

Does flood insurance cover mold?

Flood policies have their own rules about what damage they pay for. FEMA says flood coverage is separate from standard homeowners coverage and typically takes 30 days to become active.

Can I start remediation before the adjuster visits?

Photograph everything first and keep receipts. Insurers generally expect you to limit damage, but ask before removing large amounts of material.

Who pays for mold testing?

It depends on the policy. Some include testing in the mold limit and some do not. Ask your insurer before you hire an assessor.

What if my insurer says the mold was gradual?

Ask for the reasons in writing. Dated photos, readings and plumber records can help show the timeline.
