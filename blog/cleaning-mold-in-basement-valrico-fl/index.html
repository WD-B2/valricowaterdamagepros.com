How to Clean Mold in a Basement or Lower Level Room

Small patches on hard surfaces can often be cleaned. Porous materials and big areas are another matter.

By the Valrico Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 12, 2026.

24/7 dispatch
(813) 738-0233 Call Now

Searches for basement mold cleaning reach Florida homeowners too, even though true basements are rare here. Most Florida homes sit on slabs or raised foundations, so this guide applies to any below-grade or enclosed lower space, including garages, utility rooms, walkout levels, sunken rooms and crawl-space access closets. We make no claim about how common basement mold is in Hillsborough County.

The goal is simple. Clean what can be cleaned, remove what cannot and fix the moisture that caused it. Skipping the last step guarantees a return visit.

Decide whether to clean or call

EPA guidance for buildings suggests that small areas of mold, roughly less than 10 square feet, can often be handled without professionals if you protect yourself and fix the moisture source. Larger areas, repeated growth or growth inside walls usually call for expert help.

What you seeUsual choice
A few small spots on tile, glass or sealed concreteClean it yourself with care.
Patches on painted drywall under a square foot or twoOften cleanable, though removal may be safer.
Fuzzy growth on carpet, padding or insulationRemove and discard.
Large areas or growth in many roomsCall a professional.
Growth after sewage or flood waterCall a professional.
Growth inside walls or ceilingsCall a professional.

Wear protective gear and keep children and pets away while you work.

Protect yourself first

  • N95 or better respirator rated for particles.
  • Goggles that seal around the eyes.
  • Long rubber gloves and clothing you can wash right away.
  • Open windows or use a fan that exhausts outdoors.
  • Close doors to rooms you are not cleaning.
  • Avoid dry brushing or sweeping that kicks up dust.

We make no health claims about mold. If anyone in the home has health concerns, ask a physician before you start.

How to clean by material

Tile, glass and sealed concrete

Wipe with detergent and water, then dry completely. Rinse cloths often and bag them afterward.

Painted drywall

Light surface growth may wipe off, but if paint bubbles or the wall is soft, cut the drywall out.

Wood framing

Surface growth on structural wood may be cleaned and dried, but soft or rotted wood needs a carpenter.

Carpet, padding and insulation

Remove and discard. These porous materials hold moisture and growth.

Spraying bleach on porous materials does not solve the problem. A mold remediation team can explain how containment and removal work when the area is large.

Fix the moisture or it returns

Find the source

Look for leaking pipes, roof leaks, condensation on ducts and wet walls.

Stop the water

Repair the leak or drainage problem before cleaning.

Dry the space

Use dehumidifiers and air movement until surfaces and materials are dry.

Monitor

Check humidity with a meter and watch for new growth over the next weeks.

Our structural drying page explains how moisture is measured. If the damp came from a flood, see flood damage restoration.

When to stop and call a professional

  • The patch grows after you clean it.
  • There is a musty smell with no visible growth.
  • Water or sewage caused the problem.
  • Growth covers a large area or multiple rooms.
  • You find soft or rotted wood.
  • You or a family member have health concerns.

A professional will contain the area, remove affected materials and clean surfaces with HEPA filtration. They will also help you find the moisture source so it does not return.

Tools that help a small cleanup go smoothly

Keep a few simple items on hand. A moisture meter or a hygrometer tells you whether the room is still damp. Heavy trash bags hold removed material, and a HEPA vacuum is safer than a regular shop vacuum, which can blow fine particles back into the air.

Work from the cleanest area toward the dirtiest, and change gloves and wash your hands before you leave the work area.

According to the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation, controlling moisture comes first, and that guidance is why this guide keeps returning to drying and humidity.

Local note on licensing

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation licenses mold assessors and mold remediators as individuals, not as companies. A company that does both needs a separate license for each service. If you hire help, ask for the license of the person who will do the work. Homes in Dover and Tampa follow the same state rules.

Mold cleaning questions Valrico homeowners ask

Do Florida homes really have basements?

Few do, because the water table is high. Most Valrico homes sit on slabs or raised foundations. The same advice applies to garages, utility rooms and any below-grade space.

Can I paint over mold after cleaning?

Only after the surface is clean and dry and the moisture is fixed. Painting over damp or contaminated drywall hides the problem and it often returns.

How do I know the mold is gone?

The surface should look clean, smell neutral and stay dry. A professional can confirm with an inspection or clearance check if you need documentation.

Do I need to test for mold before cleaning?

Not usually when you can see growth and know the water source. Testing may be required for a sale, a lender or an insurer.

Should I use bleach?

Bleach can lighten stains on hard surfaces but does not fix porous materials or moisture. Detergent and water work on hard surfaces, and porous materials usually need removal.
