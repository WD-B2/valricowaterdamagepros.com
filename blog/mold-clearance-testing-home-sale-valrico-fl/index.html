Mold Clearance Testing for a Home Sale or Loan in Florida

A buyer or lender may ask for proof that mold work is finished. Here is how clearance works in Florida.

By the Valrico Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 12, 2026.

24/7 dispatch
(813) 738-0233 Call Now

Mold shows up at the worst moments. A buyer orders an inspection, a lender reads the report, or a seller discovers a stain while packing. Suddenly there is a deadline and a term nobody has heard before, clearance testing.

This guide explains the process in plain terms. We are a restoration company, not a lender, an attorney or an assessor, and we make no promises about what a buyer or lender will accept.

What clearance testing means

After mold has been remediated, a clearance check confirms that the work area is clean and the problem has been resolved. It usually involves a visual inspection, moisture readings and sometimes air or surface sampling. The result is a written report.

The point is independent confirmation. Someone other than the person who did the work looks at the result, which is why Florida separates the roles.

Florida keeps assessors and remediators apart

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation licenses mold assessors and mold remediators under Chapter 468, Part XVI, and Rule 61-31. They are licensed as individuals, not as businesses. A company that offers both services needs a separate license for each.

Under section 468.8419 of the Florida Statutes, an assessor may not remediate a structure that the same assessor or company assessed within the last 12 months. The reverse also applies, so a remediator cannot assess a structure it remediated in that period. Florida has no board for this, and DBPR itself regulates.

What buyers, lenders and insurers may ask for

  • A written assessment before work starts.
  • A scope of work from the remediator.
  • A clearance report from an independent assessor.
  • Moisture readings showing the area is dry.
  • Proof that the cause of moisture was fixed.
  • Receipts and photos for the file.

Each party sets its own requirements. A lender may ask for different paperwork than a buyer does, so ask them what they need before you spend money. We cannot promise acceptance and we do not claim to know how a particular lender will respond.

A sensible order of work

Find and stop the moisture

Repair the leak and dry the area first.

Independent assessment

An assessor documents what is there and where.

Remediation

A separate remediator contains and removes the mold.

Clearance check

The assessor verifies the result and writes the report.

If water caused the problem, structural drying comes first and mold remediation follows. Each step needs documentation so the file is ready for a closing.

Costs and timing during a sale

Testing, remediation and repairs each add time. Drying can take days and the clearance report may take a few more. If a closing date is close, tell your agent and the lender early so they can adjust.

We explain the scope and price of our work before it starts, and you decide before we begin. Ask each professional for a written scope so you can compare.

A realistic timeline before closing

Plan for drying, remediation and clearance to happen in sequence, with a few days between steps. Start as soon as the issue is found, tell your agent and lender what you are doing and keep every report. A clean paper trail often matters as much as the clearance result itself.

Ask your agent early how local buyers and lenders treat mold reports, since expectations differ from one transaction to the next.

According to the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation, controlling moisture comes first, and that guidance is why this guide keeps returning to drying and humidity.

Mold clearance questions Valrico sellers ask

Can the same company test and remove mold?

Florida limits this. An assessor may not remediate a structure it assessed within the previous 12 months, and the reverse applies. A company that does both needs a separate license for each service.

How long does clearance take?

The inspection can be quick, but lab results for samples take time. Ask the assessor for a timeline and share it with your agent and lender.

Will a clearance report guarantee my sale?

No. Buyers, lenders and insurers set their own standards. A clear report helps show the problem was addressed.

Do I need to disclose past mold to a buyer?

Disclosure rules are a legal question. Talk to your real estate agent or an attorney. Keep records of any past water events and repairs.

Who licenses mold professionals in Florida?

The Department of Business and Professional Regulation licenses assessors and remediators as individuals. Ask any professional for the license of the person who will do the work.
