If an appliance is leaking, turn off its water supply valve and unplug it only if the cord and floor are dry. Then call our 24/7 line. While you wait, pull the appliance out if it is safe, mop up surface water to slow spread, and move rugs and boxes off the floor.

Which appliances cause the most damage

Appliance leaks are usually slow and hidden. A cracked hose behind a washer or a loose fitting under a dishwasher can run for days before a stain or smell appears.

Dishwashers

Door seals, supply lines and drain hoses can all fail and wet cabinets and subfloor.

Washing machines

Burst hoses and overflowing drains can release large volumes quickly.

Refrigerators

Ice maker lines and defrost drains leak behind and under the unit.

Water softeners and filters

Fittings and tanks can leak steadily in utility closets and garages.

Water that spreads under cabinets and flooring in a kitchen often calls for kitchen water damage restoration. Laundry room leaks can reach adjoining bedrooms through shared walls and floors.

What we check after an appliance leak

  • The subfloor beneath and around the appliance.
  • Cabinet bases and toe kicks.
  • Drywall behind and beside the unit.
  • Flooring in adjoining rooms.
  • Ceilings below if the appliance is upstairs.
  • Supply lines and shutoff valves for ongoing leaks.

Moisture meters and infrared cameras find damp areas you cannot see. We map the wet zone before we decide what to open and dry.

Prevention that saves a cleanup

  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided lines.
  • Shut off the washer supply when you travel.
  • Check dishwasher and ice maker connections each year.
  • Pull out the fridge and look for moisture once a year.
  • Use drain pans and leak sensors under appliances.

Leak sensors cost little and warn you through a phone app or alarm. They are most useful in rooms nobody visits daily.

What to photograph before cleanup

Take a wide photo of each room, then close shots of the appliance, the supply hose or the drain, and any standing water. Photograph the model plate and the date if you can read it, because insurers sometimes ask about the age of a failed part.

Does insurance cover appliance leaks?

Many policies cover sudden leaks, but gradual leaks and wear can be excluded. Read your policy and ask your insurer. We document the damage so you have the records.

Do you repair the appliance?

No. An appliance technician or plumber handles that. We remove water, dry the area and track moisture.

Can I keep using the appliance?

Not until the leak is fixed. Turn off its water supply and wait for a repair.
