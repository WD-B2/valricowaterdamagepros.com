Air conditioners pull water out of humid air, and that water has to drain. When the condensate line clogs, the pan overflows, or the coil freezes and thaws, water spills into closets, ceilings and floors. Turn the system off at the thermostat, then call our 24/7 line so we can start drying.

Why AC leaks are common in Valrico

Humid Florida air gives an air conditioner a lot of water to remove. The condensate line carries that water outside, and algae and debris can clog it. Once the line stops, the drain pan fills and overflows.

Air handlers in attics and closets are worst. The leak drips through the ceiling or soaks closet carpet, often while the home is empty.

Where AC water usually ends up

Attic air handlers

Overflow soaks insulation and drips through ceilings below.

Closet units

Water spreads into carpet and baseboards in hallways and bedrooms.

Garage units

Water reaches slab edges, cabinets and adjoining walls.

Ductwork

Condensation on cold ducts drips onto drywall and insulation.

Ceiling stains from an attic unit often need ceiling water damage repair. Closet and hallway carpet may need carpet water damage restoration.

Preventing the next leak

  • Have the condensate line flushed on a regular schedule.
  • Install a float switch that shuts the system off on overflow.
  • Check the drain pan for water after hot humid days.
  • Keep the area around the air handler clear for inspection.

A quick check before you call

Walk to the air handler and look at the drain pan and the small drain pipe that exits near the unit. Standing water in the pan, slime around the pipe or a safety switch that has tripped all point to a clogged condensate line.

Do not pour chemicals into the line without checking with your HVAC technician. Switch the system off so more condensate does not form while you wait, and keep rugs and boxes away from the wet area so they do not soak up the overflow.

AC leak questions from Valrico homeowners

Does a water leak from my air conditioner count as an emergency?

Yes if it is soaking ceilings or floors. Turn off the system and call our 24/7 line, which is answered every day including nights and weekends.

Will insurance cover AC leak damage?

Sudden overflows may be covered, but gradual leaks and maintenance issues are sometimes excluded. Read your policy and talk to your insurer.

Do you fix the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC technician repairs the system. We remove the water, dry the structure and document the damage.

How long does drying take?

Usually several days depending on how much insulation, drywall and flooring got wet.
