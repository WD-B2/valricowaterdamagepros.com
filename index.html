Wet carpet, stained ceiling or water on the floor?

Most calls begin with something simple you can see. A ceiling stain that is growing, carpet that squishes underfoot, a puddle under the dishwasher, or a musty smell that will not go away. Each one points to a different source and a different first step.

If the stain is overhead, start with ceiling water damage repair. If water is standing on a floor, water extraction is the first move. If you hear water running in a wall or the meter keeps spinning, read about burst pipes and slab leaks. If drains are backing up, go straight to sewage backup cleanup.

DIY or professional

What towels and fans cannot do

Do it yourself

  • Removes visible water only.
  • No way to measure what is still wet.
  • Leaves moisture in walls and subfloors.
  • Odors and stains can come back weeks later.

Professional restoration

  • Pumps and extractors remove far more water.
  • Meters find hidden moisture.
  • Controlled drying with daily readings.
  • Documentation for your claim.

Some small spills are fine to handle yourself, and we say so when that is the honest answer. The line is usually drawn at water that has soaked into carpet pad, drywall or subfloors, because that moisture is the part you cannot see. Local conditions

Why the wet season matters in Valrico

The National Weather Service in Tampa Bay lists the wet season as May 25 through October 10. In that stretch, roof leaks, overflowing low spots, blocked drains and air conditioner drain lines all become more likely, and hurricane season overlaps it.

On October 11, 2024, Hillsborough County reported National Weather Service flood warnings for the Alafia River from US 301 to Lithia Pinecrest Road after Hurricane Milton and told residents along the river to seek higher ground. Valrico homeowners know that storms can turn a quiet afternoon into an emergency.

For repairs after a flood or storm, Hillsborough County Development Services handles permits. Cosmetic repairs such as drywall replacement do not need a permit, but rebuilding or structural repair in a Special Flood Hazard Area does. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the county before you start.

Questions we hear

Is your emergency line really answered 24 hours a day?

Yes. Phones are answered around the clock, every day, including nights, weekends and holidays. When you call, dispatch takes the address and what you see, then sends a technician as soon as one is available.

What should I do before help arrives?

Stop the water at its source if it is safe, move small items off the floor and photograph the damage. Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

Will I know the cost before work starts?

Yes. We explain the scope and how the price is set before work starts, and you decide before any equipment goes down.

Do you work with my insurance company?

We document the loss with photos and moisture readings that you can give to your insurer. You report the claim, and your insurer decides coverage.

How do I know the house is dry?

Moisture readings on affected materials are compared with readings on unaffected materials. When they match, drying is complete.
