Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Valrico, FL

Cabinets, countertops and subfloors take water differently. Drying them right keeps the kitchen usable.

Kitchens have more water sources than any other room, and more materials that react badly to moisture. If a sink, dishwasher or supply line has leaked, turn off the supply valve and call our 24/7 line. We dry cabinets, toe kicks, subfloors and wall bottoms before swelling and mold set in.

Where kitchen water goes

Water from a sink or dishwasher runs under the cabinets first. Toe kicks and cabinet bases are often particleboard that swells and crumbles when wet. The subfloor under the cabinets and appliances stays wet long after the surface dries.

Cabinet bases

Particleboard swells and loses strength, while solid wood and plywood survive better.

Subfloor and underlayment

Water trapped beneath flooring keeps moisture in the structure.

Drywall behind cabinets

Wall bottoms wick water upward and hide damage.

Flooring

Tile can hide water in grout lines, while wood and laminate swell and cup.

What can be saved and what usually cannot

Solid wood and plywood cabinets can often be dried. Particleboard bases that have swollen usually have to be replaced. Granite and quartz countertops are rarely harmed, but the cabinets beneath them are.

MaterialUsual outcome
Solid wood cabinetsOften dried in place with toe kicks removed.
Particleboard cabinet basesOften replaced when swollen or crumbling.
Tile floorCleaned and dried, with moisture checked under it.
Laminate and vinyl plankOften removed if water got under it.
HardwoodDried with specialized equipment where possible.

Outcomes depend on how long materials stayed wet and what they are made of.

How we dry a kitchen

Stop and extract

Supply is off and standing water is removed from the floor and cabinet bases.

Open access

Toe kicks come off and small holes allow air into cabinet cavities and wall bottoms.

Dry in place

Air movers, small drying mats and dehumidifiers run with readings.

Verify

Readings confirm dryness before cabinets and flooring are reset or replaced.

What affects the cost

  • The size of the kitchen and how many cabinet runs are wet.
  • Whether water reached the subfloor or the room next door.
  • Cabinet material and whether bases can be dried.
  • Flooring type and whether it can stay in place.
  • How long the water was present before discovery.
  • Plumbing or appliance repair, which is a separate trade.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and you decide before we begin. Photograph the leak, the cabinets and the floor before cleanup. The Florida Department of Financial Services advises reporting claims promptly and contacting your insurer before signing a repair contract.

If you notice a musty smell under the sink days later, call again. Hidden moisture behind cabinets is common and worth checking.

Small steps that protect cabinets right away

Pull the contents out of the wet cabinet and set them on a dry counter. Prop open cabinet doors so air can circulate and wipe the inside surfaces. If you can reach the toe kick, remove it so air can move into the base while you wait for a crew.

Kitchen water damage questions from Valrico homeowners

Is a kitchen leak an emergency?

If water is spreading or the source is running, yes. Our 24/7 line is answered every day including nights and weekends.

Can I keep using the kitchen?

Usually yes after the water is off and standing water is removed, but drying equipment takes space and makes noise.

Do I have to replace my cabinets?

Not always. Solid wood and plywood often dry well. Swollen particleboard usually has to be replaced.

Will the countertops be damaged?

Stone and solid surface rarely are. The cabinet bases and subfloor beneath them take the damage.

Will I know the cost before work starts?

Yes. We explain the scope and how the price is set before work starts, and you decide before we begin.
