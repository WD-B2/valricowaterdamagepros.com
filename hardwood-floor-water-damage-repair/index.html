What water does to hardwood

Wood absorbs water and swells. When the underside is wetter than the top, boards cup, with edges higher than the center. When the top is wetter, boards crown, with the center higher than the edges. Long exposure can lead to buckling, where boards lift away from the subfloor.

Cupping

Edges rise as moisture collects under the boards.

Crowning

The center of the board rises, often after drying or sanding too early.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor in severe cases.

Gaps and staining

Boards shrink after drying, leaving gaps, and dark stains can mark long exposure.

How we dry a wood floor

Fans alone rarely reach moisture trapped below the boards. We use floor drying systems that pull air through and under the floor, plus dehumidifiers to keep the surrounding air dry.

Measure

Moisture meters read the boards and subfloor, and we compare them to dry areas.

Remove water

Standing water is extracted and surface moisture is cleared.

Dry with controlled airflow

Floor drying systems and dehumidifiers run with daily readings.

Decide on repair

Once boards are dry, we see which can be refinished and which must be replaced.

Wait before you sand or refinish

Sanding or sealing a floor before it is fully dry traps moisture and often brings back cupping. A good rule is to wait for readings to stabilize at normal levels, which can take days to weeks.

Many floors that cup during a leak flatten out as they dry. Refinishing then restores the surface without replacing much wood.

What hurts a wet floor

  • Waiting several days before calling.
  • Using heat guns or space heaters to speed drying.
  • Sealing or refinishing too early.
  • Running fans without any readings.

What helps

  • Starting extraction and drying quickly.
  • Using meters to guide airflow and humidity.
  • Waiting for stable readings before repair.
  • Replacing only boards that cannot recover.

Hardwood floor questions from Valrico homeowners

Can a cupped hardwood floor be saved?

Often yes if it is dried quickly and the subfloor is dry. Many floors flatten as moisture leaves.

How long does hardwood take to dry?

From several days to a few weeks depending on thickness, finish and moisture level. Readings tell us when it is ready.

Is engineered flooring different?

Yes. Engineered boards have layers that can separate when wet. They often dry less well than solid wood.

Do I need to replace the whole floor?

Not always. We often replace only the boards that cannot recover and refinish the rest.

Do you respond at night or on weekends?

Yes. Our line is answered 24 hours a day, every day, so floors that get wet at night get early attention.
